The Monday AI for ED Drop — Issue #11: Social Media for ED: What Works and What Wastes Time

July 21, 20264 min read

Most economic development social media follows a predictable pattern: job announcements, ribbon cuttings, event reminders, and grant deadline alerts. That content is not wrong, but it is not differentiated. Here is how to use AI to build a social presence that actually grows audience, attracts deal flow, and positions your organization as an authority.

This Week’s Tips

Tip 1

Build a Monthly Content Theme Calendar

Random posting is the enemy of consistent audience growth. Start by building a monthly content framework. Prompt your AI tool: “Create a monthly social media content theme calendar for an economic development organization. Each week should have a primary theme tied to ED work (e.g., workforce, business climate, infrastructure, success stories, regional data). For each theme, suggest three post formats: a data-driven insight, a human interest story angle, and an engagement question. Give me a 12-month framework with variety and logical seasonal timing.” One morning of planning gives you a year of direction. Fill the calendar quarterly instead of scrambling for content daily.

Tip 2

Turn One Announcement Into a Week of Content

A major announcement—a new employer, a grant win, a project milestone—should power a week of content, not a single post. When you have a new announcement, prompt: “Turn this announcement into five social posts for different platforms and audiences: (1) LinkedIn for business and site selectors emphasizing investment story, (2) Facebook for community members emphasizing local jobs and impact, (3) Twitter/X for journalists emphasizing the news angle, (4) an Instagram caption with a visual direction suggestion, (5) a short LinkedIn article hook that expands the story.” Five pieces of content from one event is not over-posting—it is smart distribution.

Tip 3

Use Data to Drive Engagement, Not Just Bragging

ED organizations have access to compelling data that the public never sees: employment trends, permit activity, wage growth, industry mix shifts. That data is content. Prompt your AI tool regularly: “Here is a data point about our community: [unemployment rate dropped, largest employer added 200 jobs, new square footage of commercial space permitted]. Write three ways to frame this as a social post that educates the audience rather than just promoting our organization. Make it the kind of thing someone would share because it is genuinely interesting, not because they are a stakeholder.”
