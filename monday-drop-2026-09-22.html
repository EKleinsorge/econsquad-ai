The Monday AI for ED Drop — Issue #20: Fall Planning: Set Up Q4 Before It Arrives

September 22, 20264 min read

September is the most underutilized planning month in economic development. It sits just before the quarter when most organizations do their most visible public work, their annual report, their board budget presentations, and their year-end stakeholder outreach. The organizations that show up strong in Q4 are the ones that did their planning in September. Here is how to use AI to build your fall strategy while there is still time to execute it.

This Week’s Tips

Tip 1

Run a Q3 Debrief Before You Plan Q4

Q4 planning built on top of unexamined Q3 results often perpetuates the same priorities whether they are still right or not. Before planning forward, prompt: “I am going to describe what my economic development organization worked on in Q3 and how it went. Help me identify: what delivered the most value and should get more attention in Q4; what consumed resources but delivered less than expected; and what did not happen that should have. Then help me draft three priority adjustments for Q4 based on those findings.” Honest Q3 reflection is the foundation of useful Q4 planning. AI helps you think through it systematically rather than reactively.

Tip 2

Map Your Q4 Stakeholder Moments Now

Q4 is full of high-visibility stakeholder moments: board meetings, budget presentations, annual report releases, year-end events, and elected official briefings. Each one is an opportunity or a risk, depending on how prepared you are. Prompt: “Help me build a Q4 stakeholder calendar. Here are the key stakeholder interactions I expect between October and December. For each one, help me identify: what story I need to tell, what data I need to have ready, what decisions need to be made before the meeting, and what I need to prepare at least two weeks in advance.” A stakeholder calendar built now prevents the scramble that makes Q4 feel reactive instead of strategic.

Tip 3

Identify Your Year-End Win and Build Toward It

The best Q4s have a target: one specific outcome that you want to be able to announce or demonstrate by the end of the year. It could be a deal closed, a grant submitted, a strategic plan adopted, or a BRE program milestone. Identifying that target in September gives you 13 weeks to build toward it intentionally. Prompt: “Based on where my organization is right now, help me identify the single most achievable and most impactful goal I could accomplish by December 31. Then help me work backwards: what needs to happen in October, November, and December to make that outcome likely? What are the two or three things I control that could derail it, and how do I mitigate them?”
