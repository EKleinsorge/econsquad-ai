The Monday AI for ED Drop — Issue #13: Tax Credits: Finding Money You Didn’t Know You Had

August 4, 20264 min read

Tax credits are among the most powerful economic development tools available, and among the most consistently underutilized. Programs like the New Markets Tax Credit, Historic Tax Credit, Low-Income Housing Tax Credit, and Opportunity Zone investment rules can unlock significant private capital—but only if someone explains them clearly to the businesses and developers who could use them. That is where ED professionals have a real advantage, and where AI can dramatically reduce the knowledge gap.

This Week’s Tips

Tip 1

Build a Tax Credit Quick Reference for Your Territory

Every community has a different constellation of available tax incentives based on census tract designations, state programs, and federal overlay programs. Build a master reference document once and update it annually. Prompt: “Help me build a Tax Credit Reference Guide for [your community or region]. Include: (1) federal programs available nationally, (2) state-specific programs I should investigate further, and (3) geography-specific programs that depend on census tract or zone designations like Opportunity Zones, New Markets Tax Credit eligible tracts, or Historically Underutilized Business Zones. Format it as a quick-reference table with program name, administering agency, eligible uses, and typical benefit size.”

Tip 2

Explain Complex Programs in Plain Language

Most business owners have heard of programs like the R&D Tax Credit or Work Opportunity Tax Credit but do not understand how to access them. When a business in your community might qualify, offer to explain it. Prompt: “Write a plain-language explanation of the Work Opportunity Tax Credit for a business owner who has never applied for it before. Explain what it is, who qualifies to receive it, which employees create eligibility, the approximate dollar value, the application process, and what documentation is typically required. Write it at a level that a non-accountant can understand and act on.” Then share the summary in your BRE follow-up or business newsletter. You become the resource the business calls first.

Tip 3

Stack Programs for Maximum Impact

The most sophisticated economic development work involves layering multiple incentive programs on a single project to maximize the overall benefit. This requires understanding which programs can be stacked, in what order, and under what conditions. Prompt: “A developer is considering a historic building rehabilitation in a downtown census tract that qualifies for both New Markets Tax Credits and the Historic Tax Credit. What are the key considerations for structuring a deal that uses both programs? What are the compliance and timing requirements? What professionals would typically be involved in structuring such a deal?” This kind of guidance, offered proactively to a developer in your community, positions your organization as a sophisticated partner.
