The Monday AI for ED Drop — Issue #6: Grant Research Just Got a Whole Lot Faster

June 16, 20264 min read

Grant funding is one of the most time-intensive parts of economic development work. Searching agency websites, cross-referencing eligibility requirements, and matching opportunities to projects can consume entire days. This week, we are looking at how to compress that research cycle significantly.

This Week’s Tips

Tip 1

Build a Grant Radar With AI Monitoring

Keep a running document describing your community priorities: downtown revitalization, broadband expansion, workforce training, industrial site prep. Then regularly feed it to your AI tool with the prompt: “Given these community priorities, what federal programs under EDA, HUD, USDA Rural Development, or DOL would be most relevant right now? Identify programs by name and explain why each is a match.” You will get a prioritized shortlist rather than a raw inventory. Update your priorities document after each board meeting and run the exercise again. This keeps your grant radar current without constant manual research.

Tip 2

Decode Program Guides in Half the Time

Federal program guides are notoriously dense. NOFO documents can run 40 to 80 pages with complex eligibility criteria buried in subsections. Upload the full document to your AI tool and ask specific questions: “What are the match requirements?” “Can a county government apply directly, or only through a state intermediary?” “What activities are explicitly ineligible?” You get precise answers without reading every page. Better yet, ask: “Summarize this grant in plain English for a 5-minute presentation to our county commissioners.” That summary alone saves hours of translation work every grant cycle.

Tip 3

Create a Reusable Grant Profile for Your Organization

Most grant applications ask for the same organizational information: mission, service area, staff capacity, past project examples, fiscal agent details. Build a master Grant Profile document once and store it in your AI workspace. When a new application opens, prompt: “Using our Grant Profile, draft responses to sections 3, 5, and 7 of this NOFO.” The AI pre-populates from your vetted content. You review for accuracy and add project-specific details. Your first draft is done before lunch instead of at midnight before the deadline.
