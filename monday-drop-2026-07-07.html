The Monday AI for ED Drop — Issue #9: Press Releases That Actually Get Covered

July 7, 20264 min read

Announcing a new business location, a major expansion, or a grant award is one of the most visible things an ED organization does. It should generate buzz, build stakeholder confidence, and attract more investment attention. Too often, a generic press release buries the lead, misses the regional hook, and ends up as a paragraph in the business section that nobody reads. Here is how to do it differently.

This Week’s Tips

Tip 1

Lead With the Human Story, Not the Numbers

Journalists are looking for a story, not a data point. A press release that opens with “XYZ Corporation announced today a $14 million investment creating 87 full-time jobs” is technically complete but editorially forgettable. Try this instead: brief your AI tool on the project and ask: “Write three alternative opening paragraphs for this press release that each lead with a different human angle: the worker who will fill one of these jobs, the founder who chose this community, and the decade of work that made this community ready. Then I will choose the one that fits best.” Even if you stick with the numbers-forward approach, having three alternatives takes 30 seconds and often surprises you.

Tip 2

Write a Version for Every Audience

Your press release gets picked up by a business journal, a regional newspaper, and your board email. Each reader needs a different frame. After you have your core release, prompt: “Adapt this press release into three versions: (1) a regional business journal angle focused on competitive intelligence for the local business community, (2) a general audience version for the community newspaper focused on jobs and quality of life, and (3) a stakeholder brief for our board and investors that emphasizes ROI and organizational performance.” You write one press release and get three audience-ready versions. Each channel gets content that feels written for them.

Tip 3

Personalize Your Media Pitches

A generic blast to your media list is less effective than five personalized pitches to the journalists most likely to cover your story. Ask your AI tool: “Here are five journalists who cover [regional business / workforce / real estate]. Here is my press release. Write a short, personalized email pitch for each one that connects this story to their recent coverage and explains why their readers specifically would care.” You need to supply the journalist names and their recent articles, but the pitch writing happens in seconds. Personalized pitches earn meaningfully higher open and response rates.
