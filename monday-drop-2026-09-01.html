The Monday AI for ED Drop — Issue #17: RFI Responses That Get You to the Next Round

September 1, 20264 min read

The RFI is the gatekeeper of the site selection process. A company reviewing 30 community responses in a day is making fast eliminations based on clarity, completeness, and confidence. Most communities get cut not because they do not have the goods, but because their response buried the lead, answered questions that were not asked, and failed to make a clear case. Here is how AI helps you change that.

This Week’s Tips

Tip 1

Decode What the RFI Is Really Asking

RFIs are written by site consultants who are trying to screen communities against their client’s actual requirements. The stated question is often a proxy for a deeper concern. Before drafting your response, prompt your AI tool: “Here is an RFI for a [sector] project. For each question, tell me what the site consultant is likely trying to determine about a community. What are the deal-breaker conditions embedded in each question? Where should I lead with our strongest assets versus where do I need to address a potential gap proactively?” That analysis shapes a better response strategy before you write a single word.

Tip 2

Build a Pre-Populated RFI Response Library

About 70% of the content in any RFI response draws from the same community information: workforce profile, utility infrastructure, transportation access, cost of living, incentive toolbox, educational institutions. Build a master response library once, organized by question type, and keep it current quarterly. When an RFI arrives, prompt: “Here is our community response library and here are this RFI’s questions. Draft a response using our library content, adapted to match the specific language and priorities of this RFI. Flag any questions where our library does not have a ready answer so I can fill those in manually.” Your first draft is done in minutes instead of hours.

Tip 3

Close With a Differentiating Statement, Not Just Facts

Most RFI responses end with data. The ones that get to the next round end with a clear, confident statement of why this community is the right choice for this project specifically. After completing your response, prompt: “Based on everything in this RFI response, write a two-paragraph closing statement that makes an affirmative case for our community. Do not just summarize our data. State why we are specifically well-matched to this company’s stated priorities, reference one or two concrete proof points, and close with an invitation to a more detailed conversation.” A well-written closing is often the last thing a site consultant reads before ranking responses. Make it count.
