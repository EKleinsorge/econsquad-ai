The Monday AI for ED Drop — Issue #21: Workforce Intelligence: The New Competitive Edge in Site Selection

September 29, 20264 min read

In a 2025 survey of corporate site selectors, workforce availability ranked as the number one location factor for the fourth consecutive year. But “workforce availability” in 2026 means something more specific and more sophisticated than it did a decade ago. This week, we are building the workforce intelligence capability that wins competitive site selections.

This Week’s Tips

Tip 1

Go Beyond the Labor Shed: Build a Skills-Based Workforce Profile

Radius-based labor force counts are table stakes. Every community in your competitive set can produce a 50-mile drive-time workforce number. What differentiates competitive communities is skills-specific data: not just how many workers are within reach, but how many have the specific credentials, certifications, and occupational backgrounds the prospect needs. Ask your AI tool: “For a precision manufacturing company seeking workers with CNC machining, quality inspection, and mechanical assembly backgrounds, what data sources should I draw from and how should I structure a skills-based workforce profile that goes beyond standard BLS occupation codes?” Then build that profile before your next competitive RFI. When a site selector asks about workforce and you respond with occupation-specific data and local credential pipeline information, you signal a level of preparation that most communities cannot match.

Tip 2

Map Your Talent Pipeline to Prospect Needs

Site selectors increasingly want to see the workforce pipeline, not just the current supply. Community college enrollment in relevant programs, apprenticeship registrations, high school career pathway completions — this data exists in your region and most communities never assemble it into a coherent talent pipeline narrative. After gathering your pipeline data, ask: “Organize this workforce pipeline data into a two-page talent supply chain narrative that shows a site selector: (1) current skilled worker availability, (2) annual new entrants from training and education programs, (3) projected three-year supply growth, and (4) specific employer partnerships that demonstrate pipeline sustainability.” That narrative turns a workforce question from a static data point into a dynamic story about future supply — which is exactly what a company making a 10-year facility investment needs to hear.

Tip 3

Prepare for the Workforce Due Diligence Call

Site consultants increasingly conduct workforce due diligence calls with shortlisted communities before recommending a finalist. These calls are part validation, part stress test — they will ask about your numbers, push back on your assumptions, and probe for weaknesses in your workforce claims. Prepare by asking your AI: “Given this workforce profile, what are the three hardest questions a site consultant would ask to stress-test our workforce claims? For each question, provide a data-supported answer and identify any honest limitations we should acknowledge.” Preparing for skeptical questions is not pessimism — it is the difference between getting caught off-guard and having a credible, confident answer that builds trust. Site consultants remember which community directors could defend their numbers under pressure.
