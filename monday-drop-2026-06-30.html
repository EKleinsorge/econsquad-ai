The Monday AI for ED Drop — Issue #8: Building Incentive Packages That Win

June 30, 20264 min read

Incentive packages are high-stakes documents. Get them right and you land the project. Get them wrong and you either lose the deal or give away more than you needed to. This week, we look at how AI can help you model, structure, and communicate incentive offers more effectively.

This Week’s Tips

Tip 1

Model ROI Before You Make the Offer

Before committing to an incentive package, you need to know what you are buying. Start by gathering your projected investment numbers: jobs, capital investment, payroll, property tax base, and timeline. Then prompt your AI tool: “Given these project projections, model the estimated return on a [dollar amount] incentive package over 10 years, including direct tax revenue, indirect economic multiplier effects, and job quality considerations. Identify the break-even point and any conditions that would change the calculus.” This does not replace your formal economic impact model, but it gives you a sanity check before you go to your board or your legal team.

Tip 2

Research What Competing Communities Are Offering

The worst time to learn your competitor’s incentive toolkit is after you lose the deal. Before structuring your offer, prompt your AI tool: “What types of incentives are commonly available to economic developers in [your state] for projects in the [manufacturing / logistics / healthcare] sector? What programs exist at the federal, state, and local levels? What are the typical eligibility thresholds?” Combine that with public deal announcements and IEDC resources to map the competitive landscape before you make your offer.

Tip 3

Write the Incentive Narrative, Not Just the Numbers

Companies choose communities for emotional reasons as much as financial ones. An incentive package that reads like a tax code section is less compelling than one that tells a story about partnership. After drafting your numbers, prompt: “Help me write a two-page incentive narrative that frames our offer as a long-term partnership. Emphasize community commitment, workforce readiness, and the community’s track record of supporting businesses like this one. Make it feel like a welcome letter, not a contract.” That narrative wrapper around your numbers can be the difference between a finalist and a winner.
