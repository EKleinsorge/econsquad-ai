The Monday AI for ED Drop — Issue #15: Market Intelligence Without a Research Department

August 18, 20264 min read

Large state economic development agencies have research departments with dedicated analysts, access to expensive proprietary data platforms, and weeks to prepare a market intelligence brief. Most local and regional ED organizations have one generalist staff member and a BLS data bookmark. Here is how to close that gap with AI.

This Week’s Tips

Tip 1

Build a Community Data Snapshot You Can Update Quarterly

Most ED organizations pull their key community statistics once a year for their annual report and then operate the rest of the year from memory. A quarterly data snapshot changes that. Pick 10 to 15 metrics that matter most for your community story: unemployment rate, labor force participation, median wage, top employers, recent permit activity, new business formations, major lease transactions. Every quarter, update the numbers and brief your AI tool: “Here is our updated community data snapshot. Write a one-page market intelligence summary suitable for sharing with a site consultant or prospect. Identify the three strongest positive trends and any metrics that need context or explanation.” You have a fresh, professionally written intelligence brief in 20 minutes.

Tip 2

Monitor Target Industry Trends Between Deals

The best time to understand a target industry’s site selection trends is before you have a live deal, not during one. Build a monthly habit: pick one of your target sectors and prompt your AI tool: “What are the major trends affecting site selection for [advanced manufacturing / logistics / data centers / food processing] companies in the next 12 to 24 months? Which regions are winning the most projects in this sector and why? What are the top three questions a site consultant in this sector asks during initial screening?” You go into your next deal in that sector knowing the landscape, not discovering it under deadline pressure.

Tip 3

Create Competitive Intelligence on Peer Communities

Knowing how your competition positions itself is as important as knowing your own strengths. When you are preparing for a deal where you know the shortlist includes peer communities, prompt: “What is the economic development positioning strategy for [competing community]? What are their headline strengths for [the target sector]? What are their known weaknesses or gaps that a prospect might notice? What have they announced or invested in recently that affects the competitive picture?” Combine AI-generated context with your own on-the-ground knowledge and public announcement monitoring to build a real competitive picture.
